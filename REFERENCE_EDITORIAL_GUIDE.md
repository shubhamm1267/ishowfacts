# Reference-led story direction

All nine supplied videos were reviewed with full-duration video/audio analysis and manually inspected contact sheets. `ffprobe` reports **480 × 640, 3:4** for every reference. Model-generated aspect-ratio guesses were disregarded. Speech word counts are estimates, not forced-alignment measurements.

The examples cover octopuses, corporate brand relationships, lost treasure, aviation survival, limb-lengthening surgery, unusual medical conditions, a crime anecdote, exchange rates and historical toilets. They share a concrete viewer-facing premise, quick reveals, brief comic comparisons and expressive delivery. Some factual assertions in the references need separate verification; the references are style evidence, not research sources.

## Generic generation rules

Automatic topic selection now rotates eight application-selected categories: consumer/product design, money/economics, everyday history, technology, human-body science, documented mysteries/discoveries, food science, and nature/adaptations. It starts with consumer topics rather than repeatedly defaulting to natural scenery. A blank UI topic uses this rotation; an explicitly entered topic takes precedence.

Researched topic selections are recorded in `studio/jobs/.topic-history.json`, up to 60 records, including selections whose later generation may fail. The last 24 topics are excluded from automatic suggestions; normalized title matching and canonical Wikipedia URL checks catch repeat titles and redirected aliases. Selection is bounded: a single-topic suggestion may be retried three times, and a repeatedly reused source stops rather than silently repeating. Random-three mode receives three successive, different categories and checks recent-topic/source collisions too. Deleting the history resets this memory; no API-key setting or `.env` change is needed. Selected category is shown in the completed UI and recorded with the source in each job's `topic-selection.json`.

Validation: 119 backend tests and the Angular build passed. Regression checks cover the full category cycle, three-category selection, manual-topic history behavior, recent-title normalization, canonical aliases and retrying a repeated Gemini suggestion.

- Rotate everyday mysteries, consumer misconceptions, technology, food science, documented history, places and nature. Prefer a familiar subject with an unexpected explanation and a consequence over taxonomy trivia.
- Research uses bounded article text, rather than only the introduction, so explanations and examples beyond the definition can inform the story. The short timeout preserves a usable summary when the richer lookup fails.
- In one-topic mode, connect the existing three factual claims into a setup, explanation and payoff. Random mode still researches three distinct subjects separately.
- Vary the opening: specific question, viewer situation, personal stake or a sourced contradiction. Do not force the same catchphrase into every film.
- Reveal something useful by the second shot. Add one earned, clearly figurative desi comic comparison and return to the explanation. No fabricated dangers, false guarantees or generic final slogan.
- Write short, speakable Hindi clauses at a target of roughly 2.6–2.9 words/second. Keep the selected Fish voice identity consistent. Punctuation supplies brief emphasis pauses; no artificial breath tags or repeated pitch effects.
- Preserve the project's 1080 × 1440 layout, fitted Hindi title, yellow underline and video-first media pipeline. Context footage must never pretend to prove a historical event, hidden mechanism or a pictured brand's conduct.
- Keep SFX sparse and justified by an actual event or earned comic beat.

## Evidence checks

The opening's factual premise and promised explanation now require an explicit source excerpt and reviewer reasoning. Every narrated shot also requires a source excerpt from its own assigned article. Three correct metadata claims cannot excuse an unsupported spoken claim. Missing records fail validation and enter the existing bounded repair/review loop.

A separate spoken-claim challenge checks actual dialogue, especially causal leaps, lost qualifiers and unsupported payoff guarantees. Citation-only formatting errors can be repaired without rewriting an otherwise approved story; rejected semantic premises still require a story repair and another review. Hindi copy checks exclude the internal draft title, which is replaced by a separate upload-title step.

Reviewers must challenge causal leaps, universal claims, accusations and claims present in metadata but never explained in speech. Empty space in packaging, for example, does not establish that contents have shrunk over time. These checks improve review coverage; model reviews are not an accuracy guarantee or a substitute for source scrutiny.

Creative drafting, Hindi copyediting and independent verification use explicit reasoning on supported Gemini 3 / 2.5 models. The configured model and three-key fallback remain in place; thought parts are excluded from JSON parsing. Fast model-default minimal reasoning had missed causal overclaims during live review. Other direct `gemini()` calls retain their existing configuration.

The sample uses the normal UI Generate Video flow and production provider routing. No topic-specific generation script is required. Analysis artifacts and UI submission evidence are stored under `studio/jobs/reference-batch-review/`.
