# V4 — Reference-video study, audio and visual QA

This note records how the three reference Shorts supplied with the project informed the *editing grammar*, without copying their copyrighted footage or cloning a narrator.

## Frame-by-frame review of supplied reference MP4s

The videos were inspected using time-coded contact sheets (roughly one sample per second) and their audio was measured separately. Original footage is intentionally **not redistributed** in this project ZIP.

| Supplied reference | Visible editing techniques | Change to the engine |
|---|---|---|
| Birds on electric wires (~44 s) | Actual bird-on-wire evidence followed by a simple causal electrical circuit, closely timed verbal explanation | Gemini scene-level `visualRole`, subject matching, and diagram nodes revealed step by step |
| Man eating an airplane (~67 s) | Presenter-style curiosity, documentary/photo evidence, close factual captions and varied cuts | Short `hook / suspense / explain / reveal / payoff` speech roles, layout and camera variation |
| World's expensive school (~56 s) | Real campus imagery, specific claims shown alongside relevant objects, cost emphasis | Explicit entity per scene, source-based facts, semantic prop gate and story-matched backdrop |

### Narration style

The uploaded examples use brisk curiosity, short clauses, questions, suspense and a strong reveal. The previous autogenerated sample sounded comparatively flat and low. A rough acoustic audit gave its median voiced fundamental near **123 Hz**, compared with approximately **262–286 Hz** in the three reference audio mixes. These reference measurements are **not clean voice-only tracks** (music and overlapping sounds affect estimates), and an adult male typically has a different pitch range; we are matching the **rhythm, emphasis and intent**, not attempting to clone their pitch or voices.

V4 calls the Cartesia `/tts/bytes` API separately for every timed narration shot using a real, native male voice and scene-role directions:

- `hi-IN`: **Sagar** — Hindi male, energetic, selected for the supplied sample.
- `en-US`: **Daniel** — English US male.
- `en-GB`: **Archie** — English UK male.
- **Energetic**: faster hooks and payoffs, slightly slower explanation/suspense, curious/excited energy where supported.
- **Balanced**: reduced speed, more measured pacing.
- Gentle high-pass and compression are applied after concatenation. Scene durations are measured from returned WAVs to keep animations synchronized.

A real, short native-Hindi Cartesia casting test was successfully generated for Sagar and Anuj. Sagar's test median fundamental was about **170 Hz** with noticeable dynamic range; that is a reasonable adult male register, not a promise to reach the high-pitched reference voice. You can repeat a basic acoustic check with `python3 studio/tests/prosody-audit.py samples/live-popcorn-v4.mp4`.

### Visual truth and masking

- Every scene has a narrated factual entity (`subjectEntity`) and a short free-image query; Commons files are CC0/public-domain or CC BY and saved with attribution.
- Gemini sees the actual image and must confirm that the **dominant visible subject really is the requested subject**. A crab is not usable as an archerfish, and an unrelated aerial view does not explain a fish fact.
- Cutout suitability is checked before segmentation. `isnet-general-use` removes the background; mask validation and optional two-image Gemini QA check for missing fins, feet, legs and object edges.
- Jets and physical emitters attach to trusted, mask-snapped points or are replaced by neutral callouts. Props are positioned outside the subject bounding box. Unmentioned rulers/thermometers/flasks are removed.
- For invisible mechanisms, Gemini outputs 2–4 labeled nodes. The original `scripts/paper_doc.py` now animates these factual cards rather than showing a pretend photograph.
- Backgrounds come from the story (`paper / forest / underwater / sky / city / lab / space / kitchen / photo`) and do not automatically add dirt, grass or water to unrelated subjects.

## What is and is not automatic

V4 validates JSON shape, effect names, source-image licenses, subject matching and physical layout. It does **not** guarantee perfection: image retrieval can fail, external models can generate awkward or unsupported claims, and not every animation will look hand-edited. Check the 9:16 contact sheet, listen to the finished video, and fact-check the script/source before posting.

Commands: `node --test studio/tests/*.test.mjs`, `node studio/tests/offline-smoke.mjs`, and the live `LIVE_TEST_TOPIC=Popcorn ... node --env-file=studio/.env studio/tests/live-e2e.mjs` documented in `FACTS_STUDIO_README.md`.
